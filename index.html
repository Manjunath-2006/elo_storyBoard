Screen use is not a small detail.

03 / What developmental research says

It is not only how much — it is how.

A large systematic review suggests that screen-use context matters: content, background viewing, caregiver behaviour and co-use can relate differently to children’s outcomes.

These are associations, not proof of causation. The review reported small-to-moderate effect sizes and noted substantial heterogeneity and limitations in the evidence base. Its central implication is to consider the context and quality of screen use, not just total duration.

05 / What this means for ELO

Design for connection, not replacement.

The research does not prove that a companion robot improves child development or replaces childcare. It does help define a responsible design opportunity: support conversation, curiosity and parent visibility without centring the experience on continuous video or entertainment.

05
The ELO design proposition

A voice-first companion for curious minds.

ELO is designed for children aged 4–8. It combines spoken interaction, expressive eye animations, a child-oriented response filter and a parent application with child selection, live viewing and a stop control. These are product design goals and prototype capabilities — not proven developmental outcomes.

01
ConversationMicrophone and speaker-led question-and-answer interaction.
02

Positioning boundary: ELO is a complementary home companion. It is not a nanny, caregiver, preschool, daycare service, emergency response system or substitute for adult supervision. Do not claim that it reduces screen addiction, prevents developmental harm or guarantees child safety without appropriate product testing and independent evidence.

Definitions and denominators

LFPR: the share of the population in the labour force (employed plus unemployed, using the stated PLFS usual-status measure). Employed women: absolute estimated worker count for women aged 15+. Unpaid work minutes: average daily time for people aged 15–59 in unpaid domestic and care responsibilities. Screen time: parent-reported durations in the surveyed study population.

What is estimated versus directly counted?

The 6–7 million childcare-need figure is an estimate from a UNDP/Dalberg report, not a census count. The “less than 10%” provision figure is also reported as an estimate. Local childcare prices are directory-listed fee ranges and may vary by programme, admission timing and provider updates.

Research limitations

The 2025 screen-time study is cross-sectional and parent-reported, with participants from five northern Indian states. The JAMA Pediatrics review includes observational evidence and reports associations; it does not establish simple causal effects. The time-use comparison covers ages 15–59, not parents alone.

How to use this in the ELO pitch

Use one or two metrics per narrative point. Always say the geography, age range, time period and sample where available. Do not merge datasets into a single “working parents” statistic. Present ELO as a design response to an identified interaction gap, not as an intervention with proven clinical or developmental outcomes.
